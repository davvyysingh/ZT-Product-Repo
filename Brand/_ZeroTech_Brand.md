# ZeroTech — Brand, Range and Support Reference

Brand-level reference for questions that are not about one product: what ZeroTech is, who it is for, how the families and variants differ, and how to handle support requests. Product specifications, fitment and pricing live in the individual product files.

## What ZeroTech means

Zero is not the absence of something. It is the starting point of everything.

In shooting, your zero is the foundation — the single act that aligns your intention with your result. Before the hunt, before the shot, before anything, you zero. Without it, everything else is guesswork.

ZeroTech is named for that moment. Not the optic. Not the brand. The discipline — the deliberate act of a shooter who cares enough to get it right before the moment matters. *Tech* is the commitment that follows: the engineering, the glass, the reticle systems, the turret precision, everything that ensures once your zero is set, it holds.

> "ZeroTech is named for the most important thing a shooter does before they ever pull the trigger — zeroing their firearm. We built the technology to make sure that zero holds, wherever you take it."

## Where ZeroTech came from

ZeroTech's story begins before ZeroTech existed.

In 1945 James Watkins — an American soldier who enlisted at 18 following the bombing of Pearl Harbor — was discharged after serving in New Guinea and the Philippines, decorated with the Purple Heart and Bronze Star. He moved to Australia, and in 1965 founded Tasco Sales Australia (TSA) to distribute US-branded Tasco optics. Over the following six decades TSA became the largest specialty optical distributor in the southern hemisphere, supplying precision optics across defence, commercial and consumer markets. Three generations of optical knowledge, manufacturing relationships and field-tested expertise were built inside that business.

In 2017 a strategic shift by a major US brand reorganised Australian distribution, effectively removing TSA from the riflescope market it had helped build for fifty years. The response was not to retreat, but to build something better.

In 2018 ZeroTech Optics was founded — not by marketers, not by investors, but by people who had spent decades in the optical industry and knew exactly what the market needed and wasn't getting. The purpose was to give hunters, shooters and outdoor enthusiasts access to genuinely excellent optics at a price that didn't require compromise.

## What ZeroTech has built

ZeroTech launched in Australia in 2018 and was adopted faster than anyone expected. Within two years it had gained significant market share and expanded internationally. Today ZeroTech is active across every continent, with distribution partnerships in the Americas, Europe, the United Kingdom, Africa and the South Pacific.

Reach: active on every continent · authorised dealer and distributor network across 15+ countries · US HQ in Nashville, Tennessee · Australian HQ in Sydney · backed by three generations of optical manufacturing heritage.

## The families

- **Thrive — made for the hunt.** The broadest line in the range, built for hunters who want an optic they can zero once and then stop thinking about. Capped turrets and straightforward controls, specified for deer and predator hunting, range work and general rifle use.
- **Vengeance — value without compromise.** Built so that quality and value arrive together. Designed around the rifles people already own, and covering hunting, target shooting and range work in one family, without asking the shooter to pay for what they don't need.
- **Trace — built for distance.** ZeroTech's premium line and the top of the range. Built for long-range hunting, precision competition and technical field work — where the shot is a calculation before it is a reaction, and the emphasis is on measuring what you're looking at before acting on it.

The families are not a price ladder. Trace sits above the other two, but Thrive and Vengeance are a choice of purpose rather than a step up or down, and their prices overlap.

## The variants

- **Classic** — the standard build of a line, and its entry point. The established configuration, with the controls and reticles most shooters are already used to. A model name without a variant suffix is the Classic.
- **HD** — a step up in glass rather than in controls. Higher-definition glass for more contrast and better light transmission when the light drops. Zeroing, adjustment and handling are unchanged from the Classic, so the two behave identically in the field.
- **ED** — extra-low dispersion glass. ED elements keep light wavelengths in phase, which controls the colour fringing that softens a high-contrast edge. It shows most when you are working out *what* something is rather than simply that it is there.
- **Advanced** — the top precision tier of a line, carrying the reticle patterns found nowhere else in the range. Built for competition and long-range hunting, where measuring through the optic is part of the shot.

Which products carry which variant is held in the product files, not here.
